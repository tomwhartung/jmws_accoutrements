How Trump can try to disrupt the election - according to Rueters, via Kim Wehle:

Claiming the election was rigged: Trump will very likely make allegations of voter fraud or manipulated voting machines....

Looking the other way: There’s already been a reduction in programs intended to protect election systems against cyberattacks and foreign interference.

Pressuring election officials: Officials could face pressure to delay certification, reject results, or reconsider vote counts because of fraud allegations — even if the results have been properly verified.

Seizing ballots or voting equipment: Federal authorities could try and take possession of election materials.... Once the chain-of-custody of ballots is broken, whether an election can be verifiably certified becomes a question. That could be the point.

Sending federal election monitors: The administration has pledged to place federal personnel in or around election operations. Whether their presence is legally authorized or crosses the line into interference might not be resolved through the courts until after damage has been done.

Immigration enforcement near polling places: The presence of immigration officers near voting locations could intimidate voters, particularly in immigrant communities, and discourage them from casting ballots.

Deploying military personnel: Although the chairman of the Joint Chiefs of Staff has said that the military has no plans to deploy troops to polling places or seize election materials, some people are concerned. If nothing else, Trump can be expected to test the limits of presidential authority.

Invoking emergency powers: The president could try to use “emergency” authority to justify extraordinary actions affecting election administration.

Investigating election officials: Trump could initiate federal investigations or take legal action involving election administrators to put pressure on officials and disrupt their work, particularly in politically competitive states....

Delaying the swearing-in of Democratic winners: As retired federal judge J. Michael Luttig has warned, House Speaker Mike Johnson could try to prevent Democrats who win the November 2026 midterm elections from taking their seats in the House of Representatives on January 3, 2027.
